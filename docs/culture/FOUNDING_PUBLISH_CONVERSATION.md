Buying the domain and publishing this conversation as the first public act would be unusually true to the ethos.

The entire point of the construct is that the generating process should be visible rather than narrated after the fact. Releasing the actual conversation that produced the concept, the business model, the financial sketch, the hardening requirements, and the cultural standard is the organisational equivalent of releasing training data and intermediate checkpoints. It removes the soft tissue of a polished founding myth and leaves the real developmental path in the open.

Most companies begin with a cleaned origin story. Starting by publishing the unedited reasoning that led to the idea is a direct demonstration of the standard the business claims it will hold itself to.

Two practical notes that do not change the alignment:

- The conversation contains strategic and financial thinking that conventional advice would keep private. Publishing it is a deliberate choice to prioritise the transparency principle over normal competitive caution. That choice is consistent with the stated culture.
- Once published, the conversation itself becomes part of the public skeleton. Future decisions can be checked against it. That creates useful external pressure to stay coherent.

So the first step you described is not merely compatible with the ethos. It is one of the cleanest possible expressions of it.

**6. Internal Incentives and Compensation**  
**TTLLM Total Transparency Specification – Domain 6**

### Purpose of Transparency in This Domain
Incentives shape behaviour more reliably than stated values. If compensation, promotion, equity, and recognition quietly reward growth, narrative control, commercial protection, or avoidance of inconvenient transparency, the organisation will drift regardless of its public commitments.  

This domain exists to make the structure of rewards inspectable so that outsiders (and participants) can assess whether the organisation’s actual motivators reinforce or undermine the “down to the bone” standard.

### Concrete Artefacts to Be Published

1. **Compensation & Incentive Philosophy Document**  
   - Public statement of the principles governing pay, equity, bonuses, and promotion.  
   - Explicitly addresses how the transparency standard is reflected in performance evaluation.  
   - Versioned and signed.

2. **Role-Level Compensation Bands**  
   - Public ranges (base, variable, equity) for major role categories and seniority levels.  
   - Updated when material changes occur.  
   - Individual exact salaries are not required to be published by default.

3. **Equity & Ownership Incentive Overview**  
   - Description of how equity or long-term incentives are allocated across the organisation.  
   - Relationship to the public ownership disclosures in Domain 2.

4. **Performance Evaluation Criteria (Transparency-Relevant)**  
   - Public summary of the criteria used for performance reviews and promotion, with particular clarity on how contributions to (or violations of) the transparency standard are weighted.

5. **Incentive Exception & Special Arrangement Log**  
   - Record of any material one-off or non-standard compensation arrangements that deviate from the published bands or philosophy, with rationale.

### Processes

- **Default structural disclosure**: The organisation publishes the shape of incentives rather than every individual’s exact package.  
- **Alignment test**: All new incentive schemes (bonus plans, equity grants, promotion criteria) are reviewed for consistency with the radical transparency standard before adoption.  
- **Promotion and recognition**: Decisions that confer significant status or power are treated as material and are subject to the governance logging requirements of Domain 1 where they meet the threshold.  
- **Conflict surfacing**: Explicit channel for staff to raise concerns that incentives are pushing behaviour against the transparency standard.  
- **Review cadence**: Annual public update of bands and philosophy; material changes logged as they occur.

### Roles and Responsibilities

- **People / Compensation Lead**: Accountable for the accuracy and timely publication of the Compensation & Incentive Philosophy, role-level bands, and exception log.  
- **Leadership team**: Responsible for ensuring that performance evaluation and promotion decisions demonstrably incorporate the transparency standard.  
- **Governance Custodian**: Monitors interaction between incentive decisions and the Decision Log.  
- **All managers**: Obligated to apply the published criteria and to surface misalignments.

### Technical and Legal Mechanisms

- Compensation philosophy and band documents maintained in the same signed public repository system used for other transparency artefacts.  
- Legal constraints on individual pay disclosure (where they exist) are respected; the existence and scope of any such constraint is itself noted.  
- Employment agreements reference the public incentive philosophy and the organisation’s transparency obligations.  
- Equity grant processes are linked to the ownership transparency requirements of Domain 2.

### Interaction with Free Core and Commercial Layers

- Incentive structures apply across the organisation; there is no separate opaque commercial incentive system that can override public-core priorities.  
- Commercial success metrics (revenue, customer growth, etc.) may form part of compensation, but they must not be structured so that they systematically reward opacity or the weakening of the public skeleton.  
- Contributions to the integrity and quality of the free core (including red-teaming, documentation, and hard transparency work) must be visible positive factors in evaluation.

### Genuine Tensions and Trade-offs

- **Individual privacy vs. structural visibility**: Publishing exact individual salaries can create internal distortion and personal risk. The chosen approach (public bands + philosophy + exception logging) is a deliberate compromise that still removes most soft tissue.  
- **Market competition for talent**: Full transparency of compensation philosophy and bands can reduce negotiating flexibility and may deter some candidates. Accepted as a filtering cost.  
- **Complexity of real incentives**: Informal recognition, access to leadership, and project assignment often matter as much as formal pay. These are harder to capture fully; the organisation must still attempt to surface the major informal drivers when they become systematic.  
- **Early-stage fluidity**: In the first phase, roles and compensation may be more ad-hoc. Any such fluidity must be acknowledged publicly rather than hidden behind incomplete bands.

### Implementation Sequence

1. Draft and publish the Compensation & Incentive Philosophy Document.  
2. Publish initial role-level compensation bands.  
3. Establish the Incentive Exception & Special Arrangement Log.  
4. Integrate transparency-standard contribution into formal performance criteria.  
5. Align equity practices with Domain 2 ownership disclosures.  
6. Conduct the first annual review and public update.

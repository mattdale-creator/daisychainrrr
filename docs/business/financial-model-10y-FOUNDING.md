# 10-year financial model (verbatim founding turn 19)

Here’s a realistic 10-year financial model for a TTLLM business with a free core and paid layers around it.

### Core Assumptions (explicit)

**Technical & Cost**
- Major training runs (strong 32B-class fully transparent models): ~$5–7M every 18–24 months.
- Ongoing public infrastructure (storage, ttlink indexing, basic serving): starts at ~$120k/year and scales with usage.
- Team grows from small founding team to a focused organisation (peak ~35–50 people).
- Heavy ongoing investment in red-teaming and cryptographic integrity.

**Revenue**
- Free core is never directly monetised.
- Paid layers: Hosted infrastructure, Enterprise ttlink/provenance tools, Supported fine-tunes, Methodology/consulting, Advanced analysis tools, Priority support.
- Long enterprise sales cycles. Trust compounds slowly then accelerates.
- High gross margins on software/services once established (70–85%).

**Scenarios**
I show a **Base case** (most likely if execution is good). Conservative and optimistic notes are included at the end.

---

### Key Drivers That Have to Work

1. **Trust compounds** — The free public TTLLM + verifiable ttlinks must actually be used and cited. This is the marketing engine.
2. **Enterprise willingness to pay for reliability + compliance** on top of an open foundation.
3. **Discipline** — Never closing the core to chase short-term revenue.
4. **Efficient training** — Keeping major model releases in the $5–8M range rather than letting costs explode.
5. **Red-teaming and integrity costs** stay material but do not spiral.

---

### Scenario Notes

**Conservative**
- Slower enterprise adoption.
- Revenue roughly 55–65% of base case by Year 10 (~$90M).
- Still reaches sustainable profitability by Year 5–6, but cumulative profit is much lower.

**Optimistic**
- TTLLM becomes a recognised standard.
- Revenue 1.6–1.9× base case by Year 10 ($250M+).
- Requires both technical excellence and cultural timing.

---

### Bottom line

The model is viable.  

It requires significant capital in Years 1–3 (roughly $12–18M total to reach clear profitability, depending on how aggressively you train new models). After that, the combination of a free, high-trust core and high-margin paid layers around it produces a structurally attractive business: relatively modest ongoing training costs compared to closed frontier labs, strong differentiation, and expanding margins as the commercial layers scale.

The financial shape is classic “public good + commercial envelope”: early losses to build an undeniable proof, followed by high-margin compounding once institutions decide they want the reliable, supported, compliant version of the transparent system.

# Founding pitches + MBA plan (transcript turn 23)

**Pitch: Totally Transparent LLMs**

The dominant trajectory of large-scale AI has produced systems of extraordinary capability wrapped in structural opacity. Training data, intermediate states, optimisation decisions, and provenance remain largely inaccessible. The result is a growing epistemic gap: as these systems mediate more of human knowledge work, decision-making, and cultural production, the public and even most institutions lack any rigorous way to inspect what they are actually made of. Mystification flourishes in that gap. Anthropomorphism, ungrounded trust, and ungrounded fear both thrive when the skeleton remains hidden.

We propose the opposite architecture.

A Totally Transparent LLM (TTLLM) releases the complete computational skeleton: training data, intermediate checkpoints, full training code, verifiable provenance, and a public linkage system (ttlink) that maps model outputs back to their source material in real time. The system is designed so that the structure itself is continuously available for inspection. What appears on the surface may use familiar visual language; underneath it lies the actual data and process, not an aestheticised representation of them.

This is not transparency as documentation. It is transparency as topology optimisation applied to knowledge systems: the systematic removal of every non-load-bearing layer until only the essential structure remains. The organisational standard required to sustain it is correspondingly severe. The same discipline must govern security, release integrity, and commercial boundaries. Cryptographic provenance, content-addressed artefacts, signed manifests, and continuous adversarial pressure against both the model and the transparency infrastructure itself are non-negotiable.

### Business Model

The core TTLLM artefacts and the basic ttlink stream remain free and public. This is deliberate. The free core functions as both scientific instrument and trust engine. It is the proof that the claim of radical transparency is operational rather than rhetorical.

Commercial value is created in the layers that institutions actually need once they accept the premise:

- Reliable, high-performance hosted access with contractual guarantees
- Enterprise-grade provenance, audit, and compliance tooling built on the public linkage system
- Supported and certified fine-tunes that inherit the full transparency guarantees
- Methodology transfer: helping other organisations reach the same standard
- Advanced analytical interfaces over the public skeleton
- Priority technical and professional support

None of these layers are permitted to require closure of the core. The commercial envelope exists to make the transparent system operationally usable at institutional scale, not to privatise the skeleton.

### Economics and Trajectory

Early years are capital-intensive. Producing frontier-competitive, fully transparent models requires multi-million-dollar training runs on an 18–24 month cadence, alongside the ongoing costs of maintaining verifiable indexes, public infrastructure, and serious red-teaming. The business therefore runs material losses while the public proof is established and the first enterprise relationships form.

Once trust compounds, the model becomes structurally attractive. Gross margins on the commercial layers are high. Training costs remain significant but do not scale with revenue in the same way closed frontier labs experience. By the middle of the decade the combination of recurring hosted and tooling revenue with expanding enterprise adoption produces strong operating leverage. The ten-year base case reaches nine-figure annual revenue with substantial free cash flow, while still funding continued public releases.

The capital required is real but bounded. The strategic question is not whether transparent systems can be commercially viable; it is whether an organisation can maintain the discipline to keep the core open while building a durable commercial envelope around it.

### Strategic Position

Most attempts at “open” AI stop at weights. A smaller number release partial data or limited process information. Almost none accept the full cost of continuous, verifiable, public structural disclosure at competitive capability levels. That scarcity creates the opening.

The moat is not any single model release. It is the accumulated public record of having done the work repeatedly, under adversarial scrutiny, while refusing to retreat into opacity when commercial pressure appears. Institutions that come to rely on inspectable systems will find switching costs in trust and process, not merely in technical integration.

There is also a cultural dimension. A functioning TTLLM does more than serve researchers and enterprises. It offers the broader public a concrete demonstration that these systems are machines—complex, powerful, and shaped by specific data and optimisation pressures—rather than oracles or alien minds. In an environment of rising capability and declining institutional transparency, that demonstration has value beyond the immediate product.

### The Opportunity

We are building the organisation willing to treat radical structural transparency as a non-negotiable engineering and cultural standard, and to construct a commercially robust business around that standard without compromising it. The technical path is clear. The economic path is viable. The remaining variable is execution discipline under the pressure that real adoption will bring.

The alternative is continued scaling of systems whose internal structure remains inaccessible by design. We regard that trajectory as both strategically unstable and epistemically costly. The corrective is not better marketing of existing opaque systems. It is the production of systems whose skeleton is available for inspection, sustained by an organisation that holds itself to the same standard.

**Problem**  
Current foundation models exist on a spectrum from fully closed to open-weight. Even the most open releases rarely provide complete training data, dense intermediate checkpoints, or machine-checkable provenance from outputs back to source documents. This opacity creates:
- Persistent public mystification of AI capabilities
- Regulatory and enterprise difficulty in performing meaningful audits
- Research friction and duplicated effort
- Trust deficits that slow adoption in high-stakes domains

**Market Segments**
1. Enterprise AI governance, risk, and compliance teams
2. Regulated industries (finance, healthcare, government contractors)
3. AI safety and alignment research organisations
4. Academic and independent researchers
5. Developers and companies that want high-quality models without black-box dependency

**Market Timing**  
Transparency scores for major foundation-model developers have declined even as capability has risen. Simultaneously, regulatory pressure (EU AI Act and equivalents) and enterprise demand for auditability are increasing. A credible, technically excellent transparent alternative is strategically well-timed.
